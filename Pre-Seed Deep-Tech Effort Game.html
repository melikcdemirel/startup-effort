Where should a pre-seed deep-tech startup put its energy?

A founding team splits each month between building the technology, raising money and talking to customers. Grant rivals, investors and customers all react to what it does. The simulation plays that game thousands of times and searches for the split that most often reaches a fundable company.

    The three games inside

    Model assumptions and limits
    • The numbers are illustrative. Rates, cheque sizes and thresholds are reasonable guesses, not fitted to data. Trust the ranking of strategies and the shape of the curves more than any single percentage, and move the sliders to match your own situation.
    • Technology: progress slows as TRL rises and costs more. Past TRL 3.5, up to 70% of progress is wasted if the team does not know what the customer needs.
    • Customers: listening has diminishing returns, so a little goes a long way. Pushing before a demo exists (TRL 5) burns trust in proportion to how hard you push, and selling pushes 1.5 times harder than listening. Trust recovers slowly because deep-tech buyer pools are small.
    • Government money: non-dilutive, best suited to TRL 2–5, paid three months after a win. Your odds depend on your effort relative to the rivals' effort.
    • Investors: see a noisy signal built from TRL (40%), market knowledge (20%), contracts (25%) and grants won (15%). They fund above a bar they set from how often startups with that signal succeed. Angels fund slightly below the bar at harsher terms. A rejected pitch costs goodwill.
    • Industry pilots: need about TRL 4.5, trust and market knowledge. Each one pays cash, teaches you the market, and takes 10% of the team's time for four months.
    • Half of the field you compete in adapts toward whatever works. The other half is a fixed mix of the six named strategies.
    • Left out: team quality, IP, regulation, co-founder dynamics, and anything after the milestone. Because the model stops at the milestone, cash in the bank at that point has no value, which understates what a seed round is for.
